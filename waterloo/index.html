3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Waterloo's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Waterloo has set 49 standing record lows, fewer than the 74 that chance alone would give, and 97 record highs, more than the 74 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (88). Waterloo's all-time high is 112°F, on July 13, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

No clear change in Waterloo's highs or lows

Across 129 complete years: Waterloo's average low shows no clear change: +0.07°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.15) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. Waterloo's average high shows no clear change: −0.03°F per decade, a range (−0.12 to +0.07) that includes zero. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1930s summers were the hottest in it.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Waterloo has gotten wetter

Waterloo's yearly precipitation has risen +0.34 inches per decade (95% range +0.03 to +0.66), about +4.4 inches since 1896. The 1991–2020 normal is 36.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 2018 (54.15 in); the driest, 1910 (17.35 in).
