3 · The record book

Scottsbluff is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Scottsbluff's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Scottsbluff has set 46 standing record lows, fewer than the 74 that chance alone would give, and 141 record highs, far more than the 74 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (62). Scottsbluff's all-time high is 110°F, on July 11, 1939.

4 · How it's changed

Scottsbluff's nights have warmed. Its days have warmed since 1986.

Across 122 complete years: Scottsbluff's average low has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.13 to +0.25), about +2.5°F since 1893. Scottsbluff's average high shows no clear change: +0.04°F per decade, a range (−0.06 to +0.14) that includes zero. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.45°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.72) and highs have risen +0.81°F per decade (95% range +0.32 to +1.29). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.
