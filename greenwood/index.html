3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Greenwood's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Greenwood has set 126 standing record lows, close to the 125 that chance alone would give, and 187 record highs, more than the 125 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (98). Greenwood's all-time high is 106°F, on August 30, 2000.

4 · How it's changed

Greenwood's days got warmer. Its nights didn't.

Across 74 complete years: Greenwood's average low shows no clear change: +0.08°F per decade, a range (−0.05 to +0.20) that includes zero. Greenwood's average high has risen +0.33°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.50), about +2.5°F since 1949.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Greenwood's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.26 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 51.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1989 (79.97 in); the driest, 1965 (32.31 in). Greenwood almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.2 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
