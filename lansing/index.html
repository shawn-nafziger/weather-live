3 · The record book

Lansing has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Lansing's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Lansing has set 11 standing record lows, far fewer than the 61 that chance alone would give, and 68 record highs, close to the 61 that chance alone would give. The 1890s hold the most record highs of any decade (46). Lansing's all-time high is 103°F, on July 6, 2012.

4 · How it's changed

Lansing's days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 157 complete years: Lansing's average low has risen +0.36°F per decade (95% range +0.29 to +0.42), about +5.7°F since 1864. Lansing's average high has risen +0.12°F per decade (95% range +0.06 to +0.17), about +1.9°F since 1864.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Lansing's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.16 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 33.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1883 (48.41 in); the driest, 1930 (18.50 in).
