3 · The record book

Milwaukee has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Milwaukee's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Milwaukee has set 6 standing record lows, far fewer than the 63 that chance alone would give, and 87 record highs, more than the 63 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (55). Milwaukee's all-time high is 105°F, on July 24, 1934.

4 · How it's changed

Milwaukee has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 155 complete years: Milwaukee's average low has risen +0.17°F per decade (95% range +0.10 to +0.24), about +2.7°F since 1871. Milwaukee's average high has risen +0.28°F per decade (95% range +0.22 to +0.34), about +4.3°F since 1871.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Milwaukee has gotten wetter

Milwaukee's yearly precipitation has risen +0.32 inches per decade (95% range +0.12 to +0.52), about +4.9 inches since 1871. The 1991–2020 normal is 34.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 1876 (50.36 in); the driest, 1901 (18.69 in).
