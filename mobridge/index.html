3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Mobridge's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Mobridge has set 51 standing record lows, fewer than the 92 that chance alone would give, and 90 record highs, close to the 92 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (89). Mobridge's all-time high is 116°F, on July 16, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Mobridge's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 97 complete years: Mobridge's average low has risen +0.22°F per decade (95% range +0.10 to +0.34), about +2.5°F since 1911. Mobridge's average high shows no clear change: −0.06°F per decade, a range (−0.23 to +0.11) that includes zero. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1930s summers were the hottest in it.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Mobridge's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.10 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 17.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 2019 (29.51 in); the driest, 1934 (6.15 in).
