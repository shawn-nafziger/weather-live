3 · The record book

Laredo has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Laredo's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Laredo has set 14 standing record lows, far fewer than the 91 that chance alone would give, and 117 record highs, more than the 91 that chance alone would give. The 1990s hold the most record highs of any decade (64). Laredo's all-time high is 115°F, on June 17, 1908.

4 · How it's changed

Laredo's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 92 complete years: Laredo's average low has risen +0.37°F per decade (95% range +0.29 to +0.45), about +4.5°F since 1903. Laredo's average high shows no clear change: +0.10°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.20) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Laredo's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.08 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 18.9 inches a year. The wettest year was 1971 (42.28 in); the driest, 2011 (6.65 in). Laredo almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
