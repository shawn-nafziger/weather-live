3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Rhinelander's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Rhinelander has set 67 standing record lows, close to the 84 that chance alone would give, and 78 record highs, close to the 83 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (68). Rhinelander's all-time high is 108°F, on July 13, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Rhinelander's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 106 complete years: Rhinelander's average low has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.08 to +0.30), about +2.2°F since 1908. Rhinelander's average high shows no clear change: +0.01°F per decade, a range (−0.08 to +0.11) that includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Rhinelander's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.11 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 30.7 inches a year. The wettest year was 2019 (43.15 in); the driest, 2003 (15.32 in).
