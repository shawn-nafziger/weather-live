3 · The record book

Jacksonville is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Jacksonville's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Jacksonville has set 92 standing record lows, more than the 63 that chance alone would give, and 140 record highs, far more than the 63 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (62). Jacksonville's all-time high is 104°F, on July 28, 1872.

4 · How it's changed

Jacksonville's nights cooled over the long run but have warmed since 1986. Its days have warmed.

Across 154 complete years: Jacksonville's average low has fallen −0.27°F per decade (95% range −0.32 to −0.21), about −4.1°F since 1872. Jacksonville's average high has risen +0.20°F per decade (95% range +0.16 to +0.24), about +3.0°F since 1872. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.43°F per decade (95% range +0.07 to +0.80) and highs have risen +0.42°F per decade (95% range +0.11 to +0.72). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Jacksonville's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.10 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 53.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 1947 (82.27 in); the driest, 1927 (30.44 in). Jacksonville almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
