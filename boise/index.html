November 13: warmer than 99% of years

The high of 68°F was the 2nd warmest November 13 high (tied) of 152 since 1875, the warmest since 1999. The normal high for the date is 50°F, and the record is 69°F (1939). The low of 43°F was the 9th warmest November 13 low (tied) of 150 since 1877.

3 · The record book

Record highs keep coming. Record lows have nearly stopped.

If Boise's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Boise has set 10 standing record lows, far fewer than the 66 that chance alone would give, and 105 record highs, far more than the 65 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (40). Boise's all-time high is 111°F, on July 12, 1898.

4 · How it's changed

Boise has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 147 complete years: Boise's average low has risen +0.20°F per decade (95% range +0.13 to +0.27), about +2.9°F since 1878. Boise's average high has risen +0.15°F per decade (95% range +0.10 to +0.21), about +2.3°F since 1878.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Boise's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.10 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 11.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 1896 (22.97 in); the driest, 1966 (6.64 in).
