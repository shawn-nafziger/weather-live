3 · The record book

Poughkeepsie has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Poughkeepsie's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Poughkeepsie has set 40 standing record lows, far fewer than the 103 that chance alone would give, and 111 record highs, close to the 103 that chance alone would give. The 1940s hold the most record highs of any decade (64). Poughkeepsie's all-time high is 104°F, on August 1, 1933.

4 · How it's changed

Poughkeepsie's nights have warmed. Its days have warmed since 1983.

Across 92 complete years: Poughkeepsie's average low has risen +0.14°F per decade (95% range +0.02 to +0.25), about +1.3°F since 1931. Poughkeepsie's average high shows no clear change: +0.11°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.22) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1983, lows have risen +0.94°F per decade (95% range +0.68 to +1.21) and highs have risen +0.83°F per decade (95% range +0.51 to +1.14). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Poughkeepsie's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.25 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 42.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 2011 (62.01 in); the driest, 1964 (24.52 in).
