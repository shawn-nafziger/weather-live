3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Astoria's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Astoria has set 40 standing record lows, fewer than the 71 that chance alone would give, and 92 record highs, more than the 71 that chance alone would give. The 1940s hold the most record highs of any decade (43). Astoria's all-time high is 101°F, on July 1, 1942.

4 · How it's changed

Astoria's nights cooled over the long run but have warmed since 1986. Its days show no clear change.

Across 129 complete years: Astoria's average low has fallen −0.07°F per decade (95% range −0.12 to −0.01), about −0.9°F since 1889. Astoria's average high shows no clear change: +0.05°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.11) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.34°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.59) and highs show no clear change. The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Astoria has gotten drier

Astoria's yearly precipitation has fallen −0.97 inches per decade (95% range −1.52 to −0.41), about −13.2 inches since 1889. The 1991–2020 normal is 70.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1933 (114.03 in); the driest, 1985 (41.58 in).
