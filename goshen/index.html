3 · The record book

Goshen has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Goshen's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Goshen has set 28 standing record lows, far fewer than the 89 that chance alone would give, and 84 record highs, close to the 89 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (75). Goshen's all-time high is 111°F, on July 14, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Goshen's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 102 complete years: Goshen's average low has risen +0.36°F per decade (95% range +0.28 to +0.45), about +4.0°F since 1916. Goshen's average high shows no clear change: −0.04°F per decade, a range (−0.13 to +0.06) that includes zero. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1930s summers were the hottest in it.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Goshen has gotten wetter

Goshen's yearly precipitation has risen +0.85 inches per decade (95% range +0.49 to +1.21), about +9.3 inches since 1916. The 1991–2020 normal is 39.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 1990 (52.73 in); the driest, 1930 (22.51 in).
