3 · The record book

Hilo has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Hilo's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Hilo has set 40 standing record lows, far fewer than the 127 that chance alone would give, and 158 record highs, close to the 127 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (88). Hilo's all-time high is 94°F, on May 20, 1966.

4 · How it's changed

Hilo has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 75 complete years: Hilo's average low has risen +0.32°F per decade (95% range +0.24 to +0.41), about +2.4°F since 1950. Hilo's average high has risen +0.24°F per decade (95% range +0.13 to +0.36), about +1.8°F since 1950.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Hilo's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−2.90 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 120.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 1990 (211.22 in); the driest, 2025 (58.74 in). Hilo almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
