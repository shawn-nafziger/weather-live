3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Yakima's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Yakima has set 88 standing record lows, fewer than the 122 that chance alone would give, and 168 record highs, more than the 122 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (84). Yakima's all-time high is 113°F, on June 29, 2021.

4 · How it's changed

Yakima has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 79 complete years: Yakima's average low has risen +0.34°F per decade (95% range +0.20 to +0.48), about +2.7°F since 1947. Yakima's average high has risen +0.29°F per decade (95% range +0.13 to +0.46), about +2.3°F since 1947.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Yakima's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.11 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 8.0 inches a year. The wettest year was 1996 (14.78 in); the driest, 1976 (4.18 in).
