3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Baker City's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Baker City has set 118 standing record lows, close to the 99 that chance alone would give, and 131 record highs, more than the 99 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (62). Baker City's all-time high is 109°F, on August 10, 2018.

4 · How it's changed

Baker City's days got warmer. Its nights didn't.

Across 98 complete years: Baker City's average low shows no clear change: −0.13°F per decade, a range (−0.25 to +0.00) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. Baker City's average high has risen +0.26°F per decade (95% range +0.14 to +0.37), about +2.5°F since 1928.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Baker City has gotten drier

Baker City's yearly precipitation has fallen −0.17 inches per decade (95% range −0.34 to −0.00), about −1.6 inches since 1928. The 1991–2020 normal is 9.0 inches a year. The wettest year was 1978 (15.76 in); the driest, 2020 (3.36 in).
