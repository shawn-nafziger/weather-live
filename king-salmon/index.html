3 · The record book

King Salmon is setting record highs far faster than chance

If King Salmon's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, King Salmon has set 98 standing record lows, close to the 98 that chance alone would give, and 166 record highs, far more than the 98 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (99). King Salmon's all-time high is 89°F, on July 4, 2019.

4 · How it's changed

King Salmon has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 86 complete years: King Salmon's average low has risen +0.30°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.51), about +3.2°F since 1919. King Salmon's average high has risen +0.46°F per decade (95% range +0.25 to +0.66), about +4.8°F since 1919.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

King Salmon's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.17 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 21.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 1935 (31.55 in); the driest, 1959 (9.77 in).
