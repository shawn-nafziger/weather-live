3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Flint's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Flint has set 75 standing record lows, close to the 93 that chance alone would give, and 100 record highs, close to the 93 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (85). Flint's all-time high is 108°F, on July 8, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Flint's nights have warmed. Its days have warmed since 1986.

Across 104 complete years: Flint's average low has risen +0.10°F per decade (95% range +0.00 to +0.20), about +1.1°F since 1921. Flint's average high shows no clear change: +0.02°F per decade, a range (−0.10 to +0.14) that includes zero. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1930s summers were the hottest in it. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.60°F per decade (95% range +0.19 to +1.01) and highs have risen +0.67°F per decade (95% range +0.22 to +1.11). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Flint has gotten wetter

Flint's yearly precipitation has risen +0.40 inches per decade (95% range +0.04 to +0.77), about +4.2 inches since 1921. The 1991–2020 normal is 31.9 inches a year. The wettest year was 1975 (45.38 in); the driest, 1963 (18.08 in).
