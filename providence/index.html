3 · The record book

Providence has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Providence's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Providence has set 24 standing record lows, far fewer than the 80 that chance alone would give, and 109 record highs, more than the 80 that chance alone would give. The 1940s hold the most record highs of any decade (48). Providence's all-time high is 104°F, on August 2, 1975.

4 · How it's changed

Providence has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 121 complete years: Providence's average low has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.13 to +0.26), about +2.3°F since 1905. Providence's average high has risen +0.29°F per decade (95% range +0.23 to +0.35), about +3.5°F since 1905.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Providence has gotten wetter

Providence's yearly precipitation has risen +1.22 inches per decade (95% range +0.88 to +1.56), about +14.6 inches since 1905. The 1991–2020 normal is 47.7 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (67.52 in); the driest, 1965 (25.44 in).
