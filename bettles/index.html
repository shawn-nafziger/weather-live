3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Bettles' climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Bettles has set 73 standing record lows, fewer than the 119 that chance alone would give, and 140 record highs, close to the 119 that chance alone would give. The 1990s hold the most record highs of any decade (69). Bettles' all-time high is 93°F, on July 6, 1986.

4 · How it's changed

Bettles has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 81 complete years: Bettles' average low has risen +0.40°F per decade (95% range +0.19 to +0.60), about +3.2°F since 1945. Bettles' average high has risen +0.69°F per decade (95% range +0.49 to +0.87), about +5.5°F since 1945.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Bettles has gotten wetter

Bettles' yearly precipitation has risen +0.56 inches per decade (95% range +0.23 to +0.89), about +4.5 inches since 1945. The 1991–2020 normal is 16.0 inches a year. The wettest year was 1965 (24.46 in); the driest, 1969 (6.88 in).
