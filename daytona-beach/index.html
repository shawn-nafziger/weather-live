3 · The record book

Daytona Beach has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Daytona Beach's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Daytona Beach has set 33 standing record lows, far fewer than the 95 that chance alone would give, and 106 record highs, close to the 95 that chance alone would give. The 1990s hold the most record highs of any decade (53). Daytona Beach's all-time high is 102°F, on June 24, 1944.

4 · How it's changed

Daytona Beach has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 100 complete years: Daytona Beach's average low has risen +0.20°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.31), about +2.0°F since 1924. Daytona Beach's average high has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.12 to +0.27), about +1.9°F since 1924.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Daytona Beach's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.31 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 51.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1953 (79.29 in); the driest, 1956 (31.36 in). Daytona Beach almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
