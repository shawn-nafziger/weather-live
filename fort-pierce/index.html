3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Fort Pierce's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Fort Pierce has set 67 standing record lows, close to the 81 that chance alone would give, and 87 record highs, close to the 79 that chance alone would give. The 1990s hold the most record highs of any decade (62). Fort Pierce's all-time high is 101°F, on June 28, 1950.

4 · How it's changed

Fort Pierce's nights have warmed since 1986. Its days warmed over the long run.

Across 91 complete years: Fort Pierce's average low shows no clear change: −0.07°F per decade, a range (−0.17 to +0.03) that includes zero. Fort Pierce's average high has risen +0.17°F per decade (95% range +0.11 to +0.23), about +2.2°F since 1901. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.89°F per decade (95% range +0.57 to +1.21) and highs show no clear change. The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Fort Pierce's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.11 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 53.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 1994 (80.24 in); the driest, 1917 (31.73 in). Fort Pierce almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
