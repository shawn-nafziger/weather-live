3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Huron's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Huron has set 36 standing record lows, fewer than the 67 that chance alone would give, and 79 record highs, close to the 67 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (74). Huron's all-time high is 112°F, on July 10, 1966.

4 · How it's changed

Huron has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 144 complete years: Huron's average low has risen +0.24°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.31), about +3.4°F since 1882. Huron's average high has risen +0.20°F per decade (95% range +0.12 to +0.29), about +2.9°F since 1882.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Huron has gotten wetter

Huron's yearly precipitation has risen +0.23 inches per decade (95% range +0.02 to +0.43), about +3.2 inches since 1882. The 1991–2020 normal is 23.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 2019 (37.30 in); the driest, 1952 (9.72 in).
