3 · The record book

Oklahoma City is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Oklahoma City's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Oklahoma City has set 40 standing record lows, fewer than the 72 that chance alone would give, and 124 record highs, far more than the 72 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (50). Oklahoma City's all-time high is 113°F, on August 11, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Oklahoma City's days got warmer. Its nights didn't.

Across 135 complete years: Oklahoma City's average low shows no clear change: +0.04°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.10) that includes zero. Oklahoma City's average high has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.12 to +0.26), about +2.5°F since 1891.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Oklahoma City has gotten wetter

Oklahoma City's yearly precipitation has risen +0.51 inches per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.84), about +6.8 inches since 1891. The 1991–2020 normal is 36.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 2007 (56.95 in); the driest, 1901 (15.74 in).
