3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If St. Paul Island's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, St. Paul Island has set 59 standing record lows, fewer than the 88 that chance alone would give, and 137 record highs, far more than the 88 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (87). St. Paul Island's all-time high is 66°F, on August 25, 1987.

4 · How it's changed

St. Paul Island has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 104 complete years: St. Paul Island's average low has risen +0.15°F per decade (95% range +0.01 to +0.28), about +1.9°F since 1893. St. Paul Island's average high has risen +0.20°F per decade (95% range +0.08 to +0.31), about +2.6°F since 1893.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

St. Paul Island's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.08 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 24.0 inches a year. The wettest year was 1964 (36.61 in); the driest, 1977 (9.82 in).
