3 · The record book

Melbourne has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Melbourne's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Melbourne has set 46 standing record lows, far fewer than the 114 that chance alone would give, and 109 record highs, close to the 114 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (55). Melbourne's all-time high is 102°F, on July 14, 1980.

4 · How it's changed

Melbourne has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 72 complete years: Melbourne's average low has risen +0.31°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.44), about +2.6°F since 1942. Melbourne's average high has risen +0.18°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.27), about +1.5°F since 1942.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Melbourne's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.14 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 52.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1947 (74.28 in); the driest, 1981 (31.97 in). Melbourne almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
