3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Kodiak's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Kodiak has set 70 standing record lows, close to the 89 that chance alone would give, and 99 record highs, close to the 88 that chance alone would give. The 1980s hold the most record highs of any decade (41). Kodiak's all-time high is 86°F, on August 16, 2019.

4 · How it's changed

Kodiak's nights have warmed since 1986. Its days warmed over the long run.

Across 101 complete years: Kodiak's average low shows no clear change: +0.07°F per decade, a range (−0.04 to +0.17) that includes zero. Kodiak's average high has risen +0.17°F per decade (95% range +0.06 to +0.29), about +1.9°F since 1916. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.49°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.89) and highs show no clear change. The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Kodiak has gotten wetter

Kodiak's yearly precipitation has risen +2.50 inches per decade (95% range +1.57 to +3.42), about +27.0 inches since 1917. The 1991–2020 normal is 78.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1998 (106.25 in); the driest, 1972 (38.33 in).
