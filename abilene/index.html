3 · The record book

Abilene is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Abilene's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Abilene has set 51 standing record lows, fewer than the 70 that chance alone would give, and 145 record highs, far more than the 70 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (87). Abilene's all-time high is 113°F, on August 21, 2024.

4 · How it's changed

Abilene's nights have warmed since 1986. Its days have warmed.

Across 140 complete years: Abilene's average low shows no clear change: +0.04°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.09) that includes zero. Abilene's average high has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.12 to +0.26), about +2.6°F since 1886. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.65°F per decade (95% range +0.36 to +0.94) and highs have risen +1.07°F per decade (95% range +0.68 to +1.46). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Abilene's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.01 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 25.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1941 (48.77 in); the driest, 1956 (9.78 in).
