3 · The record book

Record highs keep coming. Record lows have nearly stopped.

If Homer's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Homer has set 16 standing record lows, far fewer than the 107 that chance alone would give, and 195 record highs, far more than the 107 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (109). Homer's all-time high is 81°F, on July 10, 1993.

4 · How it's changed

Homer has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 86 complete years: Homer's average low has risen +0.54°F per decade (95% range +0.36 to +0.72), about +4.9°F since 1933. Homer's average high has risen +0.34°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.51), about +3.1°F since 1933.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Homer's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.06 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 23.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 1981 (38.10 in); the driest, 1996 (12.95 in).
