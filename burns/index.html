3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Burns' climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Burns has set 161 standing record lows, more than the 112 that chance alone would give, and 165 record highs, more than the 112 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (85). Burns' all-time high is 107°F, on July 12, 2002.

4 · How it's changed

Burns' nights cooled over the long run. Its days have warmed.

Across 82 complete years: Burns' average low has fallen −0.48°F per decade (95% range −0.62 to −0.34), about −4.1°F since 1939. Burns' average high has risen +0.17°F per decade (95% range +0.01 to +0.33), about +1.5°F since 1939.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Burns' yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.16 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 10.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (18.24 in); the driest, 1949 (5.32 in).
