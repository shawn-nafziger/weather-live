3 · The record book

Evansville has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Evansville's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Evansville has set 28 standing record lows, far fewer than the 75 that chance alone would give, and 88 record highs, close to the 75 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (50). Evansville's all-time high is 111°F, on July 28, 1930.

4 · How it's changed

Evansville's nights cooled over the long run but have warmed since 1986. Its days warmed over the long run.

Across 129 complete years: Evansville's average low has fallen −0.16°F per decade (95% range −0.24 to −0.08), about −2.1°F since 1897. Evansville's average high has risen +0.13°F per decade (95% range +0.07 to +0.20), about +1.7°F since 1897. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.60°F per decade (95% range +0.28 to +0.93) and highs show no clear change. The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Evansville has gotten wetter

Evansville's yearly precipitation has risen +0.76 inches per decade (95% range +0.39 to +1.13), about +9.7 inches since 1897. The 1991–2020 normal is 47.8 inches a year. The wettest year was 2011 (70.03 in); the driest, 1930 (25.55 in).
