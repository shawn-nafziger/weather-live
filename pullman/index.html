3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Pullman's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Pullman has set 78 standing record lows, close to the 76 that chance alone would give, and 88 record highs, close to the 76 that chance alone would give. The 1990s hold the most record highs of any decade (40). Pullman's all-time high is 110°F, on August 4, 1961.

4 · How it's changed

Pullman's nights have warmed since 1984. Its days have warmed.

Across 112 complete years: Pullman's average low shows no clear change: −0.05°F per decade, a range (−0.13 to +0.02) that includes zero. Pullman's average high has risen +0.20°F per decade (95% range +0.12 to +0.27), about +2.6°F since 1894. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1984, lows have risen +0.46°F per decade (95% range +0.15 to +0.77) and highs have risen +0.40°F per decade (95% range +0.04 to +0.75). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Pullman has gotten drier

Pullman's yearly precipitation has fallen −0.36 inches per decade (95% range −0.68 to −0.04), about −4.6 inches since 1898. The 1991–2020 normal is 17.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1958 (29.85 in); the driest, 2005 (6.66 in).
