3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Westfield's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Westfield has set 267 standing record lows, close to the 265 that chance alone would give, and 307 record highs, close to the 266 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (122). Westfield's all-time high is 103°F, on July 6, 2010.

4 · How it's changed

No clear change in Westfield's highs or lows

Across 31 complete years: Westfield's average low shows no clear change: −0.12°F per decade, a range (−0.30 to +0.06) that includes zero. Westfield's average high shows no clear change: +0.16°F per decade, a range (−0.02 to +0.34) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Westfield's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.29 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 46.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (70.73 in); the driest, 2016 (29.82 in). Westfield almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 40.7 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
