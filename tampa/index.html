3 · The record book

Record highs keep coming. Record lows have nearly stopped.

If Tampa's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Tampa has set 12 standing record lows, far fewer than the 72 that chance alone would give, and 166 record highs, far more than the 72 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (84). Tampa's all-time high is 100°F, on July 27, 2025.

4 · How it's changed

Tampa has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 135 complete years: Tampa's average low has risen +0.22°F per decade (95% range +0.16 to +0.29), about +3.0°F since 1891. Tampa's average high has risen +0.21°F per decade (95% range +0.16 to +0.25), about +2.8°F since 1891.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Tampa's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.01 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 49.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 2024 (80.29 in); the driest, 1956 (28.89 in). Tampa almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
