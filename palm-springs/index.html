3 · The record book

Palm Springs has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Palm Springs' climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Palm Springs has set no standing record lows at all, far fewer than the 96 that chance alone would give, and 101 record highs, close to the 96 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (62). Palm Springs' all-time high is 124°F, on July 5, 2024.

4 · How it's changed

Palm Springs' days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 87 complete years: Palm Springs' average low has risen +0.94°F per decade (95% range +0.82 to +1.06), about +9.6°F since 1923. Palm Springs' average high has risen +0.14°F per decade (95% range +0.06 to +0.23), about +1.5°F since 1923.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Palm Springs has gotten drier

Palm Springs' yearly precipitation has fallen −0.26 inches per decade (95% range −0.50 to −0.01), about −2.6 inches since 1923. The 1991–2020 normal is 4.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 1943 (18.57 in); the driest, 2002 (0.76 in). Palm Springs almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
