3 · The record book

Record highs keep coming. Record lows have nearly stopped.

If San Jose's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, San Jose has set 6 standing record lows, far fewer than the 76 that chance alone would give, and 122 record highs, far more than the 76 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (45). San Jose's all-time high is 109°F, on September 6, 2022.

4 · How it's changed

San Jose's days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 119 complete years: San Jose's average low has risen +0.57°F per decade (95% range +0.51 to +0.64), about +7.6°F since 1893. San Jose's average high has risen +0.20°F per decade (95% range +0.15 to +0.25), about +2.6°F since 1893.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

San Jose's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.10 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 13.7 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (32.57 in); the driest, 2013 (3.80 in). San Jose almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
