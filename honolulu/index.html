3 · The record book

Honolulu has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Honolulu's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Honolulu has set 22 standing record lows, far fewer than the 115 that chance alone would give, and 95 record highs, close to the 116 that chance alone would give. The 1990s hold the most record highs of any decade (109). Honolulu's all-time high is 95°F, on September 19, 1994.

4 · How it's changed

Honolulu has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 81 complete years: Honolulu's average low has risen +0.35°F per decade (95% range +0.28 to +0.41), about +2.9°F since 1943. Honolulu's average high has risen +0.28°F per decade (95% range +0.18 to +0.38), about +2.3°F since 1943.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Honolulu has gotten drier

Honolulu's yearly precipitation has fallen −0.93 inches per decade (95% range −1.71 to −0.15), about −7.8 inches since 1941. The 1991–2020 normal is 16.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1965 (42.78 in); the driest, 1998 (4.52 in). Honolulu almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
