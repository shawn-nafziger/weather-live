3 · The record book

Leesburg has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Leesburg's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Leesburg has set 31 standing record lows, far fewer than the 146 that chance alone would give, and 204 record highs, more than the 145 that chance alone would give. The 1990s hold the most record highs of any decade (79). Leesburg's all-time high is 101°F, on August 13, 1991.

4 · How it's changed

Leesburg's days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 63 complete years: Leesburg's average low has risen +0.77°F per decade (95% range +0.62 to +0.93), about +5.1°F since 1959. Leesburg's average high has risen +0.29°F per decade (95% range +0.14 to +0.44), about +1.9°F since 1959.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Leesburg's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.78 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 45.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1959 (67.58 in); the driest, 2000 (26.47 in). Leesburg almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
