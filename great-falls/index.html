3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Great Falls' climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Great Falls has set 88 standing record lows, close to the 73 that chance alone would give, and 99 record highs, more than the 74 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (62). Great Falls' all-time high is 107°F, on July 25, 1933.

4 · How it's changed

Great Falls' nights cooled over the long run. Its days show no clear change.

Across 129 complete years: Great Falls' average low has fallen −0.12°F per decade (95% range −0.20 to −0.04), about −1.6°F since 1894. Great Falls' average high shows no clear change: −0.01°F per decade, a range (−0.11 to +0.10) that includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Great Falls' yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.01 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 14.7 inches a year. The wettest year was 1975 (25.24 in); the driest, 1904 (6.68 in).
