3 · The record book

Erie is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Erie's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Erie has set 37 standing record lows, fewer than the 64 that chance alone would give, and 102 record highs, far more than the 64 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (42). Erie's all-time high is 100°F, on June 25, 1988.

4 · How it's changed

Erie's nights have warmed since 1986. Its days have warmed.

Across 152 complete years: Erie's average low shows no clear change: +0.07°F per decade, a range (−0.00 to +0.13) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. Erie's average high has risen +0.17°F per decade (95% range +0.11 to +0.23), about +2.6°F since 1874. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.56°F per decade (95% range +0.15 to +0.97) and highs have risen +0.57°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.97). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Erie has gotten wetter

Erie's yearly precipitation has risen +0.51 inches per decade (95% range +0.27 to +0.75), about +7.7 inches since 1874. The 1991–2020 normal is 42.9 inches a year. The wettest year was 1977 (61.70 in); the driest, 1934 (23.84 in).
