3 · The record book

Record highs keep coming. Record lows have nearly stopped.

If Flagstaff's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Flagstaff has set 30 standing record lows, far fewer than the 77 that chance alone would give, and 128 record highs, far more than the 77 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (65). Flagstaff's all-time high is 97°F, on July 5, 1973.

4 · How it's changed

Flagstaff has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 125 complete years: Flagstaff's average low has risen +0.14°F per decade (95% range +0.08 to +0.21), about +1.8°F since 1899. Flagstaff's average high has risen +0.23°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.30), about +2.9°F since 1899.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Flagstaff's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.03 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 20.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 1965 (36.59 in); the driest, 2020 (9.59 in).
