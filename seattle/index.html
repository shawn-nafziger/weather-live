3 · The record book

Seattle has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Seattle's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Seattle has set 30 standing record lows, far fewer than the 120 that chance alone would give, and 160 record highs, more than the 120 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (69). Seattle's all-time high is 108°F, on June 28, 2021.

4 · How it's changed

Seattle has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 81 complete years: Seattle's average low has risen +0.52°F per decade (95% range +0.42 to +0.62), about +4.2°F since 1945. Seattle's average high has risen +0.38°F per decade (95% range +0.26 to +0.49), about +3.0°F since 1945.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Seattle's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.15 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 39.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1950 (55.14 in); the driest, 1952 (23.78 in).
