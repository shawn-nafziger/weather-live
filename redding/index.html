3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Redding's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Redding has set 54 standing record lows, fewer than the 76 that chance alone would give, and 108 record highs, more than the 76 that chance alone would give. The 1980s hold the most record highs of any decade (41). Redding's all-time high is 119°F, on July 6, 2024.

4 · How it's changed

Redding's nights cooled over the long run but have warmed since 1986. Its days have warmed.

Across 116 complete years: Redding's average low has fallen −0.08°F per decade (95% range −0.16 to −0.00), about −1.1°F since 1894. Redding's average high has risen +0.30°F per decade (95% range +0.23 to +0.37), about +3.9°F since 1894. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.80°F per decade (95% range +0.53 to +1.08) and highs have risen +0.69°F per decade (95% range +0.26 to +1.12). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Redding has gotten drier

Redding's yearly precipitation has fallen −0.84 inches per decade (95% range −1.54 to −0.15), about −10.9 inches since 1896. The 1991–2020 normal is 33.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (78.05 in); the driest, 2013 (12.80 in).
