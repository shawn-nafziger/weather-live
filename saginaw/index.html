3 · The record book

Saginaw has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Saginaw's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Saginaw has set 32 standing record lows, far fewer than the 85 that chance alone would give, and 97 record highs, close to the 85 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (78). Saginaw's all-time high is 111°F, on July 13, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Saginaw has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 114 complete years: Saginaw's average low has risen +0.22°F per decade (95% range +0.13 to +0.31), about +2.5°F since 1912. Saginaw's average high has risen +0.11°F per decade (95% range +0.01 to +0.21), about +1.2°F since 1912.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Saginaw has gotten wetter

Saginaw's yearly precipitation has risen +0.42 inches per decade (95% range +0.15 to +0.70), about +4.8 inches since 1912. The 1991–2020 normal is 32.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1928 (43.36 in); the driest, 1934 (17.56 in).
