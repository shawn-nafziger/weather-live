3 · The record book

Albany has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Albany's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Albany has set 16 standing record lows, far fewer than the 78 that chance alone would give, and 86 record highs, close to the 78 that chance alone would give. The 1910s hold the most record highs of any decade (41). Albany's all-time high is 107°F, on July 15, 1980.

4 · How it's changed

Albany's days and nights have warmed since 1982

Across 113 complete years: Albany's average low shows no clear change: −0.12°F per decade, a range (−0.24 to +0.00) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. Albany's average high shows no clear change: +0.07°F per decade, a range (0.00 to +0.13) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1982, lows have risen +1.61°F per decade (95% range +1.27 to +1.95) and highs have risen +0.60°F per decade (95% range +0.30 to +0.91). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Albany's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.25 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 46.9 inches a year. The wettest year was 1964 (71.46 in); the driest, 2011 (26.79 in). Albany almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
