3 · The record book

Duluth has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Duluth's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Duluth has set 25 standing record lows, far fewer than the 64 that chance alone would give, and 73 record highs, close to the 64 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (43). Duluth's all-time high is 106°F, on July 13, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Duluth's nights have warmed since 1986. Its days warmed over the long run.

Across 149 complete years: Duluth's average low shows no clear change: −0.01°F per decade, a range (−0.09 to +0.07) that includes zero. Duluth's average high has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.13 to +0.26), about +2.9°F since 1875. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.82°F per decade (95% range +0.31 to +1.32) and highs show no clear change. The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Duluth's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.12 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 31.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1879 (45.38 in); the driest, 1910 (18.11 in).
