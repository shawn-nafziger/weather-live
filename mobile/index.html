3 · The record book

Mobile is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Mobile's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Mobile has set 66 standing record lows, close to the 63 that chance alone would give, and 150 record highs, far more than the 63 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (65). Mobile's all-time high is 106°F, on August 26, 2023.

4 · How it's changed

Mobile's nights cooled over the long run but have warmed since 1986. Its days have warmed.

Across 153 complete years: Mobile's average low has fallen −0.15°F per decade (95% range −0.20 to −0.10), about −2.3°F since 1873. Mobile's average high has risen +0.23°F per decade (95% range +0.19 to +0.27), about +3.5°F since 1873. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.47°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.78) and highs have risen +0.61°F per decade (95% range +0.31 to +0.90). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Mobile's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.39 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 67.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1881 (92.32 in); the driest, 1938 (37.15 in). Mobile almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.2 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
