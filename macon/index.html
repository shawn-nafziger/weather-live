3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Macon's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Macon has set 82 standing record lows, close to the 76 that chance alone would give, and 109 record highs, more than the 76 that chance alone would give. The 1950s hold the most record highs of any decade (57). Macon's all-time high is 108°F, on July 13, 1980.

4 · How it's changed

Macon's nights cooled over the long run. Its days have warmed.

Across 126 complete years: Macon's average low has fallen −0.11°F per decade (95% range −0.17 to −0.05), about −1.4°F since 1900. Macon's average high has risen +0.29°F per decade (95% range +0.24 to +0.35), about +3.7°F since 1900.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Macon's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.26 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 46.9 inches a year. The wettest year was 2013 (72.67 in); the driest, 1954 (26.05 in). Macon almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.4 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
