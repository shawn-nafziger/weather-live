3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Key West's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Key West has set 38 standing record lows, fewer than the 64 that chance alone would give, and 65 record highs, close to the 64 that chance alone would give. The 1950s hold the most record highs of any decade (90). Key West's all-time high is 97°F, on July 19, 1880.

4 · How it's changed

Key West has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 151 complete years: Key West's average low has risen +0.13°F per decade (95% range +0.10 to +0.17), about +2.0°F since 1874. Key West's average high has risen +0.13°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.16), about +1.9°F since 1874.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Key West's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.17 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 40.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 1969 (62.92 in); the driest, 1974 (19.99 in). Key West almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
