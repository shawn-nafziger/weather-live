3 · The record book

Record highs keep coming. Record lows have nearly stopped.

If El Centro's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, El Centro has set 31 standing record lows, far fewer than the 96 that chance alone would give, and 148 record highs, far more than the 96 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (59). El Centro's all-time high is 122°F, on June 20, 2017.

4 · How it's changed

El Centro's nights warmed over the long run. Its days have warmed since 1982.

Across 92 complete years: El Centro's average low has risen +0.41°F per decade (95% range +0.31 to +0.51), about +3.9°F since 1927. El Centro's average high shows no clear change: +0.08°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.18) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1982, lows show no clear change and highs have risen +0.47°F per decade (95% range +0.19 to +0.76). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

El Centro's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.08 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 2.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (5.72 in); the driest, 2021 (0.02 in). El Centro almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
