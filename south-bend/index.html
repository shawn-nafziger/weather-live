3 · The record book

South Bend has nearly stopped setting record lows

If South Bend's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, South Bend has set 29 standing record lows, far fewer than the 75 that chance alone would give, and 74 record highs, close to the 75 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (60). South Bend's all-time high is 109°F, on July 24, 1934.

4 · How it's changed

South Bend's nights warmed over the long run. Its days have warmed since 1986.

Across 124 complete years: South Bend's average low has risen +0.18°F per decade (95% range +0.11 to +0.25), about +2.4°F since 1894. South Bend's average high shows no clear change: +0.05°F per decade, a range (−0.03 to +0.12) that includes zero. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1930s summers were the hottest in it. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows show no clear change and highs have risen +0.45°F per decade (95% range +0.01 to +0.90). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

South Bend has gotten wetter

South Bend's yearly precipitation has risen +0.55 inches per decade (95% range +0.27 to +0.83), about +7.1 inches since 1895. The 1991–2020 normal is 39.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1990 (55.61 in); the driest, 1917 (22.17 in).
