3 · The record book

Pine Bluff has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Pine Bluff's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Pine Bluff has set 35 standing record lows, far fewer than the 72 that chance alone would give, and 42 record highs, fewer than the 72 that chance alone would give. The 1920s hold the most record highs of any decade (88). Pine Bluff's all-time high is 112°F, on August 15, 1943.

4 · How it's changed

Pine Bluff's nights have warmed. Its days cooled over the long run but have warmed since 1983.

Across 124 complete years: Pine Bluff's average low has risen +0.14°F per decade (95% range +0.07 to +0.22), about +1.9°F since 1892. Pine Bluff's average high has fallen −0.17°F per decade (95% range −0.24 to −0.09), about −2.2°F since 1892. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1983, lows have risen +0.46°F per decade (95% range +0.07 to +0.85) and highs have risen +0.55°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.94). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Pine Bluff's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.10 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 50.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1905 (82.89 in); the driest, 2010 (28.43 in).
