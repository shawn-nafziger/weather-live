3 · The record book

San Francisco has nearly stopped setting record lows

If San Francisco's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, San Francisco has set 6 standing record lows, far fewer than the 121 that chance alone would give, and 155 record highs, more than the 121 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (57). San Francisco's all-time high is 105°F, on June 14, 2000.

4 · How it's changed

San Francisco's days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 78 complete years: San Francisco's average low has risen +0.64°F per decade (95% range +0.54 to +0.73), about +5.0°F since 1946. San Francisco's average high has risen +0.35°F per decade (95% range +0.22 to +0.47), about +2.8°F since 1946.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

San Francisco's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.02 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 19.6 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (38.34 in); the driest, 2013 (3.38 in). San Francisco almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
