3 · The record book

Palmdale has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Palmdale's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Palmdale has set 19 standing record lows, far fewer than the 104 that chance alone would give, and 144 record highs, more than the 104 that chance alone would give. The 1990s hold the most record highs of any decade (68). Palmdale's all-time high is 118°F, on August 5, 1990.

4 · How it's changed

Palmdale has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 91 complete years: Palmdale's average low has risen +0.48°F per decade (95% range +0.36 to +0.61), about +4.5°F since 1933. Palmdale's average high has risen +0.26°F per decade (95% range +0.15 to +0.36), about +2.4°F since 1933.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Palmdale's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.29 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 6.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1943 (18.98 in); the driest, 1953 (0.86 in). Palmdale almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
