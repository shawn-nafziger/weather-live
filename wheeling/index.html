3 · The record book

Wheeling has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Wheeling's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Wheeling has set 12 standing record lows, far fewer than the 78 that chance alone would give, and 47 record highs, fewer than the 79 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (86). Wheeling's all-time high is 106°F, on August 6, 1918.

4 · How it's changed

Wheeling's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 116 complete years: Wheeling's average low has risen +0.34°F per decade (95% range +0.25 to +0.44), about +4.3°F since 1900. Wheeling's average high shows no clear change: +0.00°F per decade, a range (−0.11 to +0.12) that includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Wheeling's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.22 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 38.9 inches a year. The wettest year was 2018 (57.65 in); the driest, 1963 (27.34 in).
