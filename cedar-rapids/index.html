3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Cedar Rapids' climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Cedar Rapids has set 61 standing record lows, close to the 74 that chance alone would give, and 73 record highs, close to the 74 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (57). Cedar Rapids' all-time high is 110°F, on July 6, 1911.

4 · How it's changed

Cedar Rapids' nights show no clear change. Its days cooled over the long run.

Across 130 complete years: Cedar Rapids' average low shows no clear change: +0.01°F per decade, a range (−0.06 to +0.09) that includes zero. Cedar Rapids' average high has fallen −0.10°F per decade (95% range −0.18 to −0.01), about −1.3°F since 1893. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1930s summers were the hottest in it.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Cedar Rapids has gotten wetter

Cedar Rapids' yearly precipitation has risen +0.43 inches per decade (95% range +0.11 to +0.76), about +5.7 inches since 1893. The 1991–2020 normal is 35.6 inches a year. The wettest year was 1993 (60.46 in); the driest, 1910 (18.74 in).
