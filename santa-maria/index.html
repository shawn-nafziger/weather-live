3 · The record book

Santa Maria has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Santa Maria's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Santa Maria has set 14 standing record lows, far fewer than the 85 that chance alone would give, and 114 record highs, more than the 85 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (54). Santa Maria's all-time high is 110°F, on June 20, 2008.

4 · How it's changed

Santa Maria's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 112 complete years: Santa Maria's average low has risen +0.24°F per decade (95% range +0.15 to +0.32), about +2.8°F since 1906. Santa Maria's average high shows no clear change: +0.02°F per decade, a range (−0.08 to +0.11) that includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Santa Maria's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.22 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 13.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1941 (33.51 in); the driest, 2013 (2.99 in). Santa Maria almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
