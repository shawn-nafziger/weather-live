3 · The record book

Yuma has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Yuma's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Yuma has set 2 standing record lows, far fewer than the 66 that chance alone would give, and 98 record highs, far more than the 65 that chance alone would give. The 1950s hold the most record highs of any decade (84). Yuma's all-time high is 124°F, on July 28, 1995.

4 · How it's changed

Yuma's days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 140 complete years: Yuma's average low has risen +0.50°F per decade (95% range +0.45 to +0.56), about +7.3°F since 1879. Yuma's average high has risen +0.20°F per decade (95% range +0.15 to +0.25), about +2.9°F since 1879.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Yuma's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.01 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 3.0 inches a year. The wettest year was 1926 (9.23 in); the driest, 2002 (0.03 in). Yuma almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
