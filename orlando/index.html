3 · The record book

Orlando has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Orlando's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Orlando has set 17 standing record lows, far fewer than the 75 that chance alone would give, and 50 record highs, fewer than the 75 that chance alone would give. The 1920s hold the most record highs of any decade (90). Orlando's all-time high is 103°F, on September 8, 1921.

4 · How it's changed

Orlando's days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 126 complete years: Orlando's average low has risen +0.24°F per decade (95% range +0.18 to +0.30), about +3.1°F since 1894. Orlando's average high has risen +0.07°F per decade (95% range +0.01 to +0.13), about +0.9°F since 1894.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Orlando's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.13 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 51.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 1905 (74.79 in); the driest, 2000 (30.38 in). Orlando almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
