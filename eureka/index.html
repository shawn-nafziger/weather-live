3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Eureka's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Eureka has set 88 standing record lows, more than the 70 that chance alone would give, and 99 record highs, more than the 70 that chance alone would give. The 1980s hold the most record highs of any decade (50). Eureka's all-time high is 87°F, on October 26, 1993.

4 · How it's changed

Eureka's nights have cooled since 1986. Its days warmed over the long run.

Across 139 complete years: Eureka's average low shows no clear change: +0.03°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.07) that includes zero. Eureka's average high has risen +0.25°F per decade (95% range +0.20 to +0.30), about +3.4°F since 1887. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have fallen −0.47°F per decade (95% range −0.74 to −0.21) and highs show no clear change. The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Eureka's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.12 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 40.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (67.21 in); the driest, 2013 (16.60 in). Eureka almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.1 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
