3 · The record book

Athens has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Athens' climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Athens has set 38 standing record lows, far fewer than the 78 that chance alone would give, and 81 record highs, close to the 79 that chance alone would give. The 1920s hold the most record highs of any decade (75). Athens' all-time high is 109°F, on June 29, 2012.

4 · How it's changed

Athens has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 116 complete years: Athens' average low has risen +0.12°F per decade (95% range +0.06 to +0.18), about +1.5°F since 1903. Athens' average high has risen +0.16°F per decade (95% range +0.07 to +0.25), about +1.9°F since 1903.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Athens' yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.10 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 48.9 inches a year. The wettest year was 1964 (71.39 in); the driest, 1954 (28.61 in).
