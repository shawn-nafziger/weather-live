3 · The record book

Norfolk has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Norfolk's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Norfolk has set 37 standing record lows, far fewer than the 74 that chance alone would give, and 78 record highs, close to the 74 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (88). Norfolk's all-time high is 116°F, on July 17, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Norfolk's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 123 complete years: Norfolk's average low has risen +0.24°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.31), about +3.1°F since 1894. Norfolk's average high shows no clear change: +0.04°F per decade, a range (−0.06 to +0.14) that includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Norfolk's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.06 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 27.0 inches a year. The wettest year was 2007 (37.81 in); the driest, 2022 (13.27 in).
