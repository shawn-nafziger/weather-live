3 · The record book

Hagerstown has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Hagerstown's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Hagerstown has set 4 standing record lows, far fewer than the 77 that chance alone would give, and 77 record highs, close to the 77 that chance alone would give. The 1990s hold the most record highs of any decade (50). Hagerstown's all-time high is 105°F, on July 14, 1954.

4 · How it's changed

Hagerstown's days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 118 complete years: Hagerstown's average low has risen +0.46°F per decade (95% range +0.39 to +0.54), about +5.9°F since 1899. Hagerstown's average high has risen +0.12°F per decade (95% range +0.05 to +0.19), about +1.5°F since 1899.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Hagerstown's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.09 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 39.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1996 (76.65 in); the driest, 2001 (23.13 in).
