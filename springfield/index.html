3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Springfield's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Springfield has set 64 standing record lows, close to the 66 that chance alone would give, and 90 record highs, more than the 67 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (76). Springfield's all-time high is 112°F, on July 14, 1954.

4 · How it's changed

Springfield's days got warmer. Its nights didn't.

Across 146 complete years: Springfield's average low shows no clear change: −0.05°F per decade, a range (−0.11 to +0.02) that includes zero. Springfield's average high has risen +0.15°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.22), about +2.2°F since 1880.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Springfield's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.12 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 38.0 inches a year. The wettest year was 1882 (58.21 in); the driest, 1914 (22.76 in).
