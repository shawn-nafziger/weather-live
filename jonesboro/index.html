3 · The record book

Jonesboro has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Jonesboro's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Jonesboro has set 26 standing record lows, far fewer than the 77 that chance alone would give, and 51 record highs, fewer than the 78 that chance alone would give. The 1900s hold the most record highs of any decade (77). Jonesboro's all-time high is 116°F, on July 12, 1901.

4 · How it's changed

Jonesboro's nights got warmer while its days cooled

Across 107 complete years: Jonesboro's average low has risen +0.17°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.24), about +2.1°F since 1897. Jonesboro's average high has fallen −0.23°F per decade (95% range −0.31 to −0.15), about −3.0°F since 1897. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1900s summers were the hottest in it.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Jonesboro's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.09 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 48.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 2009 (74.00 in); the driest, 1943 (27.86 in).
