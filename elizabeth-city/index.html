3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Elizabeth City's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Elizabeth City has set 68 standing record lows, fewer than the 106 that chance alone would give, and 103 record highs, close to the 106 that chance alone would give. The 1940s hold the most record highs of any decade (76). Elizabeth City's all-time high is 107°F, on July 18, 1942.

4 · How it's changed

Elizabeth City's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 88 complete years: Elizabeth City's average low has risen +0.22°F per decade (95% range +0.13 to +0.32), about +2.0°F since 1934. Elizabeth City's average high shows no clear change: +0.08°F per decade, a range (−0.05 to +0.20) that includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Elizabeth City has gotten drier

Elizabeth City's yearly precipitation has fallen −0.73 inches per decade (95% range −1.35 to −0.10), about −6.6 inches since 1934. The 1991–2020 normal is 47.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 2018 (63.95 in); the driest, 2007 (25.58 in).
