3 · The record book

Record highs keep coming. Record lows have nearly stopped.

If San Antonio's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, San Antonio has set 27 standing record lows, far fewer than the 69 that chance alone would give, and 153 record highs, far more than the 69 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (74). San Antonio's all-time high is 111°F, on September 5, 2000.

4 · How it's changed

San Antonio has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 140 complete years: San Antonio's average low has risen +0.07°F per decade (95% range +0.01 to +0.12), about +0.9°F since 1886. San Antonio's average high has risen +0.16°F per decade (95% range +0.10 to +0.22), about +2.2°F since 1886.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

San Antonio has gotten wetter

San Antonio's yearly precipitation has risen +0.43 inches per decade (95% range +0.06 to +0.80), about +6.0 inches since 1886. The 1991–2020 normal is 32.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 1973 (52.28 in); the driest, 1917 (10.11 in). San Antonio almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.2 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
