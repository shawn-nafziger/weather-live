3 · The record book

Battle Creek has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Battle Creek's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Battle Creek has set 28 standing record lows, far fewer than the 74 that chance alone would give, and 72 record highs, close to the 74 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (52). Battle Creek's all-time high is 104°F, on July 14, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Battle Creek's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 130 complete years: Battle Creek's average low has risen +0.17°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.24), about +2.2°F since 1895. Battle Creek's average high shows no clear change: +0.07°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.14) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1930s summers were the hottest in it.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Battle Creek's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.02 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 32.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1985 (47.67 in); the driest, 2005 (21.24 in).
