3 · The record book

North Platte is setting record highs far faster than chance

If North Platte's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, North Platte has set 68 standing record lows, close to the 64 that chance alone would give, and 128 record highs, far more than the 64 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (59). North Platte's all-time high is 112°F, on July 11, 1954.

4 · How it's changed

North Platte's nights cooled over the long run. Its days have warmed.

Across 151 complete years: North Platte's average low has fallen −0.13°F per decade (95% range −0.20 to −0.07), about −2.0°F since 1875. North Platte's average high has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.12 to +0.26), about +2.8°F since 1875.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

North Platte has gotten wetter

North Platte's yearly precipitation has risen +0.21 inches per decade (95% range +0.04 to +0.39), about +3.2 inches since 1875. The 1991–2020 normal is 21.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1951 (33.44 in); the driest, 1931 (10.01 in).
