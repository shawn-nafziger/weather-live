3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Kalispell's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Kalispell has set 83 standing record lows, close to the 77 that chance alone would give, and 100 record highs, more than the 77 that chance alone would give. The 1960s hold the most record highs of any decade (41). Kalispell's all-time high is 105°F, on August 4, 1961.

4 · How it's changed

Kalispell's nights cooled over the long run. Its days have warmed.

Across 123 complete years: Kalispell's average low has fallen −0.13°F per decade (95% range −0.21 to −0.06), about −1.7°F since 1900. Kalispell's average high has risen +0.24°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.32), about +3.1°F since 1900.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Kalispell has gotten wetter

Kalispell's yearly precipitation has risen +0.21 inches per decade (95% range +0.05 to +0.37), about +2.6 inches since 1900. The 1991–2020 normal is 16.7 inches a year. The wettest year was 1996 (25.23 in); the driest, 1929 (10.39 in).
