3 · The record book

Ukiah has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Ukiah's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Ukiah has set 10 standing record lows, far fewer than the 74 that chance alone would give, and 86 record highs, close to the 74 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (46). Ukiah's all-time high is 117°F, on September 6, 2022.

4 · How it's changed

Ukiah's days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 125 complete years: Ukiah's average low has risen +0.38°F per decade (95% range +0.33 to +0.44), about +5.0°F since 1893. Ukiah's average high has risen +0.14°F per decade (95% range +0.06 to +0.21), about +1.8°F since 1893.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Ukiah's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.10 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 36.4 inches a year. The wettest year was 1998 (67.03 in); the driest, 2013 (7.50 in). Ukiah almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.1 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
