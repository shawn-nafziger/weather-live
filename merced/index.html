3 · The record book

Merced has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Merced's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Merced has set 12 standing record lows, far fewer than the 80 that chance alone would give, and 97 record highs, close to the 80 that chance alone would give. The 1900s hold the most record highs of any decade (66). Merced's all-time high is 116°F, on September 6, 2022.

4 · How it's changed

Merced has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 101 complete years: Merced's average low has risen +0.21°F per decade (95% range +0.14 to +0.29), about +2.6°F since 1902. Merced's average high has risen +0.13°F per decade (95% range +0.06 to +0.21), about +1.7°F since 1902.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Merced's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.04 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 11.8 inches a year. The wettest year was 1998 (21.98 in); the driest, 2013 (3.79 in). Merced almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
