3 · The record book

Record highs keep coming. Record lows have nearly stopped.

If Madison's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Madison has set 23 standing record lows, far fewer than the 62 that chance alone would give, and 99 record highs, far more than the 62 that chance alone would give. The 1950s hold the most record highs of any decade (43). Madison's all-time high is 107°F, on July 14, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Madison's nights cooled over the long run but have warmed since 1986. Its days warmed over the long run.

Across 157 complete years: Madison's average low has fallen −0.11°F per decade (95% range −0.19 to −0.03), about −1.7°F since 1869. Madison's average high has risen +0.32°F per decade (95% range +0.26 to +0.39), about +5.1°F since 1869. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.68°F per decade (95% range +0.23 to +1.12) and highs show no clear change. The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Madison has gotten wetter

Madison's yearly precipitation has risen +0.33 inches per decade (95% range +0.10 to +0.56), about +5.1 inches since 1869. The 1991–2020 normal is 37.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1881 (52.91 in); the driest, 1895 (13.69 in).
