3 · The record book

Akron has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Akron's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Akron has set 20 standing record lows, far fewer than the 71 that chance alone would give, and 78 record highs, close to the 71 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (65). Akron's all-time high is 104°F, on August 6, 1918.

4 · How it's changed

Akron has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 134 complete years: Akron's average low has risen +0.12°F per decade (95% range +0.05 to +0.18), about +1.6°F since 1888. Akron's average high has risen +0.12°F per decade (95% range +0.05 to +0.18), about +1.6°F since 1888.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Akron has gotten wetter

Akron's yearly precipitation has risen +0.55 inches per decade (95% range +0.26 to +0.83), about +7.0 inches since 1897. The 1991–2020 normal is 41.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 1990 (65.70 in); the driest, 1963 (23.79 in).
