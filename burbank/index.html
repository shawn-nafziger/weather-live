3 · The record book

Burbank has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Burbank's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Burbank has set 7 standing record lows, far fewer than the 114 that chance alone would give, and 103 record highs, close to the 115 that chance alone would give. The 1970s hold the most record highs of any decade (76). Burbank's all-time high is 114°F, on July 6, 2018.

4 · How it's changed

Burbank's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 79 complete years: Burbank's average low has risen +0.69°F per decade (95% range +0.56 to +0.81), about +5.9°F since 1940. Burbank's average high shows no clear change: +0.14°F per decade, a range (−0.01 to +0.29) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Burbank's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.22 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 14.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (39.77 in); the driest, 2002 (0.26 in). Burbank almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
