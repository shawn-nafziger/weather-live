3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Martinsburg's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Martinsburg has set 41 standing record lows, fewer than the 73 that chance alone would give, and 54 record highs, fewer than the 73 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (67). Martinsburg's all-time high is 112°F, on July 11, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Martinsburg's days and nights both warmed. Nights warmed faster.

Across 129 complete years: Martinsburg's average low has risen +0.21°F per decade (95% range +0.15 to +0.27), about +2.8°F since 1893. Martinsburg's average high has risen +0.07°F per decade (95% range +0.00 to +0.14), about +0.9°F since 1893.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Martinsburg's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.09 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 39.7 inches a year. The wettest year was 2018 (61.58 in); the driest, 1930 (21.43 in).
