Since 2000, Gainesville has set 70 standing record lows, close to the 76 that chance alone would give, and 74 record highs, close to the 74 that chance alone would give. 4 · How it's changed

Gainesville's days and nights have warmed since 1986

Across 105 complete years: Gainesville's average low shows no clear change: −0.06°F per decade, a range (−0.13 to +0.01) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. Gainesville's average high shows no clear change: +0.04°F per decade, a range (−0.03 to +0.10) that includes zero. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.38°F per decade (95% range +0.03 to +0.72) and highs have risen +0.80°F per decade (95% range +0.53 to +1.07). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Gainesville's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.04 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 48.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1953 (73.30 in); the driest, 1917 (32.79 in). Gainesville almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
