    How to read this
    • Record lows since 2000: every calendar date has one record low. If the climate weren't changing, each year would have the same chance of holding it, so the years since 2000 would hold their fair share. Far fewer means the cold extremes have stopped coming.
    • Is the gap real? We pick the same number of years at random from the place's record, 20,000 times, and see how often they hold as few record lows. Whole years are drawn, so a cold snap that set several records at once stays together. Orange means that happened less than 2.5% of the time.
    • Nights and days: the trend in the yearly average low and high, in °F per decade, across every complete year. "Signal" means its 95% range clears zero.
    • Volunteer stations are read once a day by local observers; official city records are the long records NOAA keeps for cities, often joining several sites over the years. Neither is adjusted for station moves or new instruments.
