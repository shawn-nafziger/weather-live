3 · The record book

Sacramento is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Sacramento's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Sacramento has set 44 standing record lows, fewer than the 67 that chance alone would give, and 136 record highs, far more than the 67 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (55). Sacramento's all-time high is 115°F, on June 15, 1961.

4 · How it's changed

Sacramento's nights have warmed since 1985. Its days warmed over the long run.

Across 140 complete years: Sacramento's average low shows no clear change: −0.02°F per decade, a range (−0.06 to +0.03) that includes zero. Sacramento's average high has risen +0.32°F per decade (95% range +0.27 to +0.37), about +4.7°F since 1878. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1985, lows have risen +0.30°F per decade (95% range +0.03 to +0.57) and highs show no clear change. The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Sacramento's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.07 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 18.0 inches a year. The wettest year was 1884 (34.92 in); the driest, 2013 (5.81 in). Sacramento almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
