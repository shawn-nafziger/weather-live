How warm was every day since 1900 in Valentine?

Since 1900, Valentine’s daily high has been above the 1991–2020 normal for the date on 48% of 46,351 days. That share was 45% in the 1900s and is 60% in the 2020s so far.

The latest day on the chart, November 26, 2026, had a high of 59°F, 14° above the normal of 45°F for the date.

Each row is a year and each square a day, colored by how far the day’s high was from the normal for that date: red warmer, blue cooler, dark close to normal. Hover or tap any square to read it. A new square fills in every morning.

    Has the typical day in Valentine warmed?

    In the 1900s, 45% of days beat today’s normal high. In the 2010s, 53%; in 2020–2025, 59%. Where the rows turn redder toward the bottom, the typical day has warmed.

    Which years ran warmest and coolest?

    Of 126 complete years since 1900, 2012 ran warmest: its highs averaged 5.3°F above normal, and 70% of its days were above normal. 1951 ran coolest: its highs averaged 6.4°F below normal, with 33% of days above.

    How long can a warm or cool spell last?

    The longest run of above-normal highs lasted 33 days, from December 13, 1986, to January 14, 1987. The longest run below normal lasted 43 days, from November 18, 1983, to December 30, 1983.

    So far in 2026, through November 26, 64% of days have had a high above normal, the 3rd-highest share for that stretch of the year in 127 years since 1900.

    How to read the chart

    Read down the chart to move through the years and across it to move through the seasons. Each square is one day: red if its high was warmer than the normal high for that date, blue if cooler, and darker the closer it was to normal. Gaps are days with no reading (and February 29 in years that don’t have one). The thin column at the right is each year’s average. Hover over any square or the year column, or tap it on a phone, for the details.

    “Normal” is fixed for every year: the 1991–2020 average high for each date, smoothed over 15 days. So the early rows aren’t compared with their own era’s normal but with today’s: where the rows turn redder toward the bottom, the typical day has warmed, and where they don’t, it hasn’t.

    Where the numbers come from

    Daily highs come from Valentine area (ThreadEx; Miller Field), NOAA’s official record, via the Regional Climate Centers’ ACIS service, in whole degrees Fahrenheit. The normal for each date is the 1991–2020 average of that date’s highs in the same record, smoothed with a 15-day window. A day counts as above normal if its high is warmer than that normal, even by a fraction of a degree.

    This is the official city record as kept, without adjustments. It joins several observing sites over the years, and a move, a new instrument or the city growing around a station can shift readings (city growth mostly warms nights); NOAA's adjusted climate datasets correct for these. Treat any single number as approximate.
