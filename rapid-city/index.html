3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Rapid City's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Rapid City has set 122 standing record lows, close to the 116 that chance alone would give, and 162 record highs, more than the 116 that chance alone would give. The 2000s hold the most record highs of any decade (60). Rapid City's all-time high is 112°F, on July 25, 2026.

4 · How it's changed

Rapid City's days got warmer. Its nights didn't.

Across 83 complete years: Rapid City's average low shows no clear change: −0.02°F per decade, a range (−0.15 to +0.11) that includes zero. Rapid City's average high has risen +0.28°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.47), about +2.3°F since 1943.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Rapid City's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.09 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 17.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 2019 (28.43 in); the driest, 1974 (9.12 in).
