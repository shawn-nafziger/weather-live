3 · The record book

Lawton is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Lawton's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Lawton has set 62 standing record lows, fewer than the 89 that chance alone would give, and 149 record highs, far more than the 88 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (63). Lawton's all-time high is 115°F, on August 11, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Lawton's nights warmed over the long run. Its days have warmed since 1970.

Across 93 complete years: Lawton's average low has risen +0.12°F per decade (95% range +0.05 to +0.19), about +1.4°F since 1913. Lawton's average high shows no clear change: +0.01°F per decade, a range (−0.11 to +0.12) that includes zero. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1970, lows show no clear change and highs have risen +0.78°F per decade (95% range +0.35 to +1.20). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Lawton has gotten drier

Lawton's yearly precipitation has fallen −0.53 inches per decade (95% range −0.99 to −0.07), about −5.9 inches since 1913. The 1991–2020 normal is 27.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1919 (48.04 in); the driest, 2003 (14.25 in). Lawton almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.6 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
