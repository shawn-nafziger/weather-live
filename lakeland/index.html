3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Lakeland's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Lakeland has set 121 standing record lows, close to the 125 that chance alone would give, and 150 record highs, close to the 125 that chance alone would give. The 1980s hold the most record highs of any decade (116). Lakeland's all-time high is 105°F, on June 4, 1985.

4 · How it's changed

Lakeland's days and nights both warmed. Days warmed faster.

Across 75 complete years: Lakeland's average low has risen +0.21°F per decade (95% range +0.08 to +0.34), about +1.6°F since 1949. Lakeland's average high has risen +0.62°F per decade (95% range +0.49 to +0.75), about +4.7°F since 1949.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Lakeland has gotten wetter

Lakeland's yearly precipitation has risen +1.64 inches per decade (95% range +0.68 to +2.60), about +12.5 inches since 1949. The 1991–2020 normal is 56.9 inches a year. The wettest year was 2018 (78.15 in); the driest, 1961 (35.83 in). Lakeland almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
