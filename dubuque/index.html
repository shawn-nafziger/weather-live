3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Dubuque's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Dubuque has set 70 standing record lows, close to the 64 that chance alone would give, and 52 record highs, close to the 64 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (76). Dubuque's all-time high is 110°F, on July 14, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Dubuque's nights cooled over the long run. Its days show no clear change.

Across 151 complete years: Dubuque's average low has fallen −0.09°F per decade (95% range −0.16 to −0.02), about −1.4°F since 1875. Dubuque's average high shows no clear change: −0.06°F per decade, a range (−0.12 to +0.01) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1930s summers were the hottest in it.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Dubuque has gotten wetter

Dubuque's yearly precipitation has risen +0.36 inches per decade (95% range +0.07 to +0.65), about +5.4 inches since 1874. The 1991–2020 normal is 38.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1961 (63.39 in); the driest, 1894 (19.35 in).
