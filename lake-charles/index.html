3 · The record book

Lake Charles has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Lake Charles' climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Lake Charles has set 32 standing record lows, far fewer than the 76 that chance alone would give, and 75 record highs, close to the 76 that chance alone would give. The 1910s hold the most record highs of any decade (138). Lake Charles' all-time high is 109°F, on August 27, 2023.

4 · How it's changed

Lake Charles' nights have warmed. Its days have warmed since 1986.

Across 113 complete years: Lake Charles' average low has risen +0.24°F per decade (95% range +0.17 to +0.32), about +3.2°F since 1896. Lake Charles' average high shows no clear change: −0.02°F per decade, a range (−0.09 to +0.04) that includes zero. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1910s summers were the hottest in it. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.55°F per decade (95% range +0.26 to +0.85) and highs have risen +0.58°F per decade (95% range +0.33 to +0.84). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Lake Charles' yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.32 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 59.8 inches a year. The wettest year was 2002 (85.18 in); the driest, 1954 (30.08 in). Lake Charles almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.2 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
