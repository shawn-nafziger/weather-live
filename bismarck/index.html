3 · The record book

Bismarck is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Bismarck's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Bismarck has set 33 standing record lows, fewer than the 64 that chance alone would give, and 97 record highs, far more than the 64 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (44). Bismarck's all-time high is 114°F, on July 6, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Bismarck has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 151 complete years: Bismarck's average low has risen +0.17°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.24), about +2.5°F since 1875. Bismarck's average high has risen +0.31°F per decade (95% range +0.23 to +0.39), about +4.6°F since 1875.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Bismarck's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.08 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 19.0 inches a year. The wettest year was 1876 (30.92 in); the driest, 1936 (5.97 in).
