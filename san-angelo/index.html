3 · The record book

San Angelo is setting record highs far faster than chance

If San Angelo's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, San Angelo has set 49 standing record lows, fewer than the 85 that chance alone would give, and 185 record highs, far more than the 85 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (90). San Angelo's all-time high is 114°F, on June 20, 2023.

4 · How it's changed

San Angelo has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 101 complete years: San Angelo's average low has risen +0.16°F per decade (95% range +0.08 to +0.24), about +1.9°F since 1908. San Angelo's average high has risen +0.16°F per decade (95% range +0.04 to +0.28), about +1.9°F since 1908.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

San Angelo has gotten wetter

San Angelo's yearly precipitation has risen +0.80 inches per decade (95% range +0.20 to +1.41), about +6.4 inches since 1945. The 1991–2020 normal is 20.9 inches a year. The wettest year was 2016 (35.72 in); the driest, 1956 (7.41 in).
