3 · The record book

Dayton has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Dayton's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Dayton has set 25 standing record lows, far fewer than the 74 that chance alone would give, and 62 record highs, close to the 74 that chance alone would give. The 1890s hold the most record highs of any decade (54). Dayton's all-time high is 108°F, on July 22, 1901.

4 · How it's changed

Dayton's days and nights have warmed since 1986

Across 130 complete years: Dayton's average low shows no clear change: +0.05°F per decade, a range (−0.03 to +0.13) that includes zero. Dayton's average high shows no clear change: −0.06°F per decade, a range (−0.14 to +0.01) that includes zero. It's close, but the range still includes zero. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1890s summers were the hottest in it. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows have risen +0.69°F per decade (95% range +0.34 to +1.05) and highs have risen +0.62°F per decade (95% range +0.20 to +1.04). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Dayton has gotten wetter

Dayton's yearly precipitation has risen +0.38 inches per decade (95% range +0.06 to +0.71), about +4.9 inches since 1896. The 1991–2020 normal is 41.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1990 (59.75 in); the driest, 1934 (23.74 in).
