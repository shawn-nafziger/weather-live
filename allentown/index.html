3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Allentown's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Allentown has set 55 standing record lows, fewer than the 94 that chance alone would give, and 100 record highs, close to the 95 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (46). Allentown's all-time high is 105°F, on July 3, 1966.

4 · How it's changed

Allentown has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 98 complete years: Allentown's average low has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.10 to +0.27), about +1.9°F since 1925. Allentown's average high has risen +0.18°F per decade (95% range +0.08 to +0.28), about +1.8°F since 1925.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Allentown's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.32 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 47.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 2011 (71.72 in); the driest, 1980 (29.82 in).
