3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Lynchburg's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Lynchburg has set 87 standing record lows, close to the 73 that chance alone would give, and 68 record highs, close to the 74 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (73). Lynchburg's all-time high is 106°F, on July 10, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Lynchburg's nights cooled over the long run. Its days have warmed since 1986.

Across 132 complete years: Lynchburg's average low has fallen −0.11°F per decade (95% range −0.17 to −0.06), about −1.5°F since 1893. Lynchburg's average high shows no clear change: +0.02°F per decade, a range (−0.04 to +0.09) that includes zero. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1986, lows show no clear change and highs have risen +0.71°F per decade (95% range +0.34 to +1.07). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Lynchburg's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.27 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 42.7 inches a year. The wettest year was 2020 (69.73 in); the driest, 1930 (19.83 in).
