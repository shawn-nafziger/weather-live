3 · The record book

Midland-Odessa is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Midland-Odessa's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Midland-Odessa has set 53 standing record lows, fewer than the 102 that chance alone would give, and 181 record highs, far more than the 102 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (74). Midland-Odessa's all-time high is 116°F, on June 27, 1994.

4 · How it's changed

Midland-Odessa has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 95 complete years: Midland-Odessa's average low has risen +0.29°F per decade (95% range +0.19 to +0.40), about +2.7°F since 1931. Midland-Odessa's average high has risen +0.23°F per decade (95% range +0.10 to +0.35), about +2.1°F since 1931.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Midland-Odessa's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (−0.24 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 13.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 1986 (32.13 in); the driest, 1951 (4.60 in).
