3 · The record book

Record highs keep coming. Record lows have nearly stopped.

If McAllen's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, McAllen has set 42 standing record lows, far fewer than the 115 that chance alone would give, and 223 record highs, far more than the 115 that chance alone would give. The 2010s hold the most record highs of any decade (94). McAllen's all-time high is 111°F, on June 22, 2017.

4 · How it's changed

McAllen has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 82 complete years: McAllen's average low has risen +0.62°F per decade (95% range +0.52 to +0.71), about +5.1°F since 1942. McAllen's average high has risen +0.42°F per decade (95% range +0.29 to +0.56), about +3.5°F since 1942.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

McAllen's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.44 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 22.1 inches a year. The wettest year was 1966 (37.17 in); the driest, 1989 (8.64 in). McAllen almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
