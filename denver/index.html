3 · The record book

Denver is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Denver's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Denver has set 61 standing record lows, close to the 63 that chance alone would give, and 179 record highs, far more than the 63 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (65). Denver's all-time high is 105°F, on August 8, 1878.

4 · How it's changed

Denver's days got warmer. Its nights didn't.

Across 154 complete years: Denver's average low shows no clear change: −0.01°F per decade, a range (−0.07 to +0.05) that includes zero. Denver's average high has risen +0.19°F per decade (95% range +0.14 to +0.25), about +2.9°F since 1872.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Denver's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.03 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 14.2 inches a year. The wettest year was 1967 (23.31 in); the driest, 2002 (7.48 in).
