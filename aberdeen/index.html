3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Aberdeen's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Aberdeen has set 53 standing record lows, fewer than the 74 that chance alone would give, and 65 record highs, close to the 74 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (92). Aberdeen's all-time high is 115°F, on July 6, 1936.

4 · How it's changed

Aberdeen's nights got warmer. Its days didn't.

Across 126 complete years: Aberdeen's average low has risen +0.20°F per decade (95% range +0.09 to +0.30), about +2.6°F since 1893. Aberdeen's average high shows no clear change: +0.02°F per decade, a range (−0.09 to +0.13) that includes zero.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Aberdeen has gotten drier

Aberdeen's yearly precipitation has fallen −0.34 inches per decade (95% range −0.62 to −0.05), about −4.4 inches since 1895. The 1991–2020 normal is 21.8 inches a year. The wettest year was 1896 (42.11 in); the driest, 1976 (7.88 in).
