3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Caribou's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Caribou has set 63 standing record lows, fewer than the 112 that chance alone would give, and 148 record highs, more than the 112 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (58). Caribou's all-time high is 96°F, on June 29, 1944.

4 · How it's changed

Caribou has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 84 complete years: Caribou's average low has risen +0.42°F per decade (95% range +0.28 to +0.55), about +3.5°F since 1941. Caribou's average high has risen +0.42°F per decade (95% range +0.31 to +0.54), about +3.6°F since 1941.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Caribou has gotten wetter

Caribou's yearly precipitation has risen +0.79 inches per decade (95% range +0.28 to +1.29), about +6.6 inches since 1941. The 1991–2020 normal is 40.7 inches a year. The wettest year was 2011 (55.36 in); the driest, 1966 (27.92 in).
