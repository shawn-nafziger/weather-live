3 · The record book

Bangor has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Bangor's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Bangor has set 50 standing record lows, far fewer than the 100 that chance alone would give, and 107 record highs, close to the 100 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (78). Bangor's all-time high is 104°F, on August 19, 1935.

4 · How it's changed

Bangor's nights warmed over the long run. Its days have warmed since 1985.

Across 90 complete years: Bangor's average low has risen +0.21°F per decade (95% range +0.06 to +0.35), about +2.1°F since 1926. Bangor's average high shows no clear change: −0.02°F per decade, a range (−0.17 to +0.13) that includes zero. The last 40 years tell a different story: since 1985, lows show no clear change and highs have risen +0.52°F per decade (95% range +0.19 to +0.85). The full record joins different observing sites over the years, and a move (for example, from the city to a cooler airport) can hide or exaggerate a long-run trend, so the recent decades are the clearer guide here.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain and snow: no clear long-term change

Bangor's yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.40 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 41.8 inches a year. The wettest year was 1983 (65.24 in); the driest, 2001 (24.58 in).
