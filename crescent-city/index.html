3 · The record book

Crescent City has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Crescent City's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Crescent City has set 8 standing record lows, far fewer than the 79 that chance alone would give, and 30 record highs, far fewer than the 80 that chance alone would give. The 1930s hold the most record highs of any decade (109). Crescent City's all-time high is 97°F, on September 20, 1919.

4 · How it's changed

Crescent City's nights got warmer while its days cooled

Across 105 complete years: Crescent City's average low has risen +0.21°F per decade (95% range +0.16 to +0.27), about +2.8°F since 1895. Crescent City's average high has fallen −0.25°F per decade (95% range −0.36 to −0.14), about −3.2°F since 1895. July and August highs have edged down across the full record; the 1930s summers were the hottest in it.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Crescent City has gotten drier

Crescent City's yearly precipitation has fallen −1.79 inches per decade (95% range −2.69 to −0.90), about −22.9 inches since 1897. The 1991–2020 normal is 61.3 inches a year. The wettest year was 1904 (107.61 in); the driest, 2013 (28.92 in). Crescent City almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.0 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
