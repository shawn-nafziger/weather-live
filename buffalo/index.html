3 · The record book

Buffalo has nearly stopped setting record lows

If Buffalo's climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Buffalo has set 21 standing record lows, far fewer than the 64 that chance alone would give, and 95 record highs, more than the 64 that chance alone would give. The 1950s hold the most record highs of any decade (51). Buffalo's all-time high is 99°F, on August 27, 1948.

4 · How it's changed

Buffalo's days and nights both warmed. Days warmed faster.

Across 152 complete years: Buffalo's average low has risen +0.09°F per decade (95% range +0.03 to +0.15), about +1.4°F since 1874. Buffalo's average high has risen +0.31°F per decade (95% range +0.26 to +0.36), about +4.7°F since 1874.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Buffalo has gotten wetter

Buffalo's yearly precipitation has risen +0.39 inches per decade (95% range +0.18 to +0.60), about +6.1 inches since 1871. The 1991–2020 normal is 40.6 inches a year. The wettest year was 1878 (60.24 in); the driest, 1941 (22.16 in).
