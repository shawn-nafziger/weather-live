3 · The record book

Record highs and lows, by the decade that set them

If Grand Forks' climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Grand Forks has set 91 standing record lows, close to the 115 that chance alone would give, and 128 record highs, close to the 115 that chance alone would give. The 1950s hold the most record highs of any decade (48). Grand Forks' all-time high is 104°F, on August 7, 1983.

4 · How it's changed

Grand Forks has warmed, days and nights alike

Across 82 complete years: Grand Forks' average low has risen +0.38°F per decade (95% range +0.21 to +0.56), about +3.2°F since 1942. Grand Forks' average high has risen +0.33°F per decade (95% range +0.13 to +0.53), about +2.7°F since 1942.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Grand Forks has gotten wetter

Grand Forks' yearly precipitation has risen +0.34 inches per decade (95% range +0.03 to +0.65), about +2.8 inches since 1942. The 1991–2020 normal is 21.5 inches a year. The wettest year was 2019 (28.11 in); the driest, 1963 (10.65 in).
