3 · The record book

Austin (Bergstrom) is setting record highs far faster than chance

If Austin (Bergstrom)'s climate weren't changing, every year would have the same chance of holding a daily record. Since 2000, Austin (Bergstrom) has set 206 standing record lows, far more than the 121 that chance alone would give, and 234 record highs, far more than the 120 that chance alone would give. The 2020s hold the most record highs of any decade (94). Austin (Bergstrom)'s all-time high is 112°F, on September 5, 2000.

4 · How it's changed

Austin (Bergstrom)'s nights have cooled. Its days have warmed.

Across 78 complete years: Austin (Bergstrom)'s average low has fallen −0.31°F per decade (95% range −0.43 to −0.20), about −2.6°F since 1943. Austin (Bergstrom)'s average high has risen +0.36°F per decade (95% range +0.21 to +0.51), about +3.0°F since 1943.

How to read these
  • Trend: a straight line through every complete year. "Per decade" is how much it rises or falls every 10 years.
  • 95% range: how far the trend could plausibly be off, given how much years bounce around. If the whole range is above zero, the warming is real, not luck.
  • Signal, at the edge, noise: signal means the range clears zero; at the edge means it's at least 80% of the way there; noise means it doesn't come close.

5 · Rain and snow

Rain: no clear long-term change

Austin (Bergstrom)'s yearly precipitation shows no clear long-term change (+0.67 inches per decade, a range that includes zero). The 1991–2020 normal is 34.8 inches a year. The wettest year was 2015 (58.29 in); the driest, 1954 (9.98 in). Austin (Bergstrom) almost never sees snow (the 1991–2020 average is 0.2 inches a winter), so there's no snowfall chart.
